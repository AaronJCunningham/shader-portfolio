# Marjorie Cameron (1922–1995)

> *"The mixture of the occult, Eros, and sensuality is very much in one's face when you appear in front of her artwork."*
> — Tosh Berman

## Who She Was

Marjorie Cameron Parsons Kimmel — known simply as **Cameron** — was one of the most singular figures in 20th century American occultism and art. Born in Belle Plaine, Iowa in 1922, she was a US Navy WAVE in WWII, then descended into the occult underground of postwar Pasadena, California.

She met Jack Parsons — rocket scientist, JPL co-founder, and Thelemic magician — who believed she was the "elemental woman" he had invoked during the **Babalon Working**, a series of sex magic rituals he performed in early 1946. They married the same year. After Parsons died in a laboratory explosion in 1952 (Cameron always believed it was assassination), she went into the Beaumont desert alone, made art, established a multi-racial sex-magic group called The Children, and eventually settled in Los Angeles.

She became part of the LA avant-garde — friends with Kenneth Anger (she appeared in *Inauguration of the Pleasure Dome* as the Scarlet Woman), Curtis Harrington, and deep in the Beat/psychedelia/art scene of the 50s and 60s. Her drawing was arrested in 1957 when it appeared in Wallace Berman's journal *Semina* — deemed obscene. She died in 1995 in West Hollywood. Her work has since shown at the Centre Georges Pompidou, Nicole Klagsbrun Gallery, and Deitch Projects.

## The Art

Medium: ink, dye, gouache on paper and parchment. Highly idiosyncratic, impossible to categorize.

Visual language: winged figures, hermaphroditic beings, fire, serpents, celestial geometry, the Scarlet Woman, Babalon imagery, demonic faces, Enochian and Thelemic symbols woven into imagery. Dense with personal mythology. Simultaneously raw and refined — the lines feel like they come from somewhere urgent.

Her work reads as vision art — not illustration of existing symbolic systems but genuine transmissions of her own internal cosmology.

Key works and where to look:
- **Fairy Queen (1962)** — ink and dyes on parchment. One of her most famous.
- **Dark Angel** — shown at the Pompidou 2008 "Traces du Sacré"
- **Songs for the Witch Woman** — collection with Jack Parsons (exhibited at Deitch Projects 2015)

## The Magic System

### Thelema / Babalon Current
Cameron was a Thelemite — a follower of Aleister Crowley's system centered on individual Will and the law "Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the law." But her orientation was specifically through the **Babalon** current — the Scarlet Woman, the Whore of Babylon reclaimed as a goddess of liberation, fire, and erotic power.

Babalon in Thelema represents:
- The Great Mother / feminine divine principle
- Liberation from ego through sacred sexuality
- The destroyer of false selves — the cup that holds the blood of the saints

Cameron lived this mythology rather than studying it. She *was* Babalon, at least in her own cosmology. Her art expresses this from the inside.

### The Babalon Working
Parsons and Cameron (and L. Ron Hubbard, briefly) performed a months-long sex magic ritual in early 1946 intending to incarnate the spirit of Babalon in a "moonchild." Cameron arrived shortly after. The ritual is now one of the most notorious and debated acts in modern occult history. Cameron later performed her own rituals to communicate with Parsons' spirit after his death.

### Vision Art as Magical Practice
Cameron's art wasn't illustration — it was practice. She drew what she saw during magical states, visionary experiences, and grief. The process of making the image *was* the ritual. The art object is a record of contact.

This is distinct from Spare's algorithmic approach — Cameron's method was more receptive, more raw. Open the channel and let it through.

## Esoteric Context

- Initiated into Thelema through Parsons
- Mentored Kenneth Anger in Crowleyan Thelema
- Part of the Beatnik/psychedelic arts underground (Berman, Harrington, DeBrier)
- Her work appeared alongside Beat poetry, experimental film, and early psychedelic culture
- Connected to the mythology of Jack Parsons, JPL, and the Antichrist working
- The Cameron–Parsons Foundation preserves her legacy

## What This Means for the Mauve Zone

Cameron's art is about **transmission** — figures arriving from somewhere else, fire that is also a body, entities that fuse the erotic with the divine.

**Winged forms in Three.js:** Her signature imagery — winged androgynous beings, flame-shapes with faces — translates into shader-based form generation. Wings as particle emissions, figures that flicker like fire.

**Ink on parchment texture:** Her work has a specific materiality — dark ink bleeding into rough paper, dyes spreading organically. In Three.js this could be a post-processing approach: ink-bleed simulation, paper grain shader, organic spread algorithms.

**The arrival of a figure from noise:** Her images feel like something is *arriving* — condensing out of background chaos into temporary form. A Three.js sketch could express this: noise field → figure emergence → dissolution back into field.

**Color language:** Deep reds, blacks, ochres, celestial blues. Fire palette. The colors of Babalon — crimson and gold.

**Erotic geometry:** The way her figures merge and interpenetrate — bodies as intersecting forms — suggests geometry that overlaps, bleeds, passes through itself.
